# RCM Project Analysis

## Overview

This analysis evaluates fictional Revenue Cycle Management (RCM) data across a six-month period.

The analysis focuses on claim volume, clean claim performance, claim rejections, denials, accounts receivable, aged AR, collections, and overall revenue cycle performance.

The objective is to identify trends, evaluate operational performance, identify potential revenue leakage, and recommend process improvements.

## Analysis Period

January – June

## Key Performance Summary

During the six-month period, the project shows an overall improvement in several RCM performance indicators.

Total claim volume increased while the clean claim rate improved and the denial rate decreased.

At the same time, total AR and AR over 90 days declined, while collections increased.

These trends indicate improved claim quality, denial management, follow-up, and overall revenue cycle performance.

## Claim Volume Analysis

Total claims increased from 1,250 in January to 1,450 in June.

This represents an increase in claim volume while maintaining an improving clean claim rate.

Higher claim volume requires effective eligibility verification, claim validation, submission monitoring, and follow-up processes.

## Clean Claim Rate Analysis

Clean claim rate improved during the analysis period.

January: 87.20%

June: 91.03%

The improvement suggests that fewer claims required correction before payer processing.

Possible contributing factors include:

Improved eligibility verification  
Better claim validation  
Improved coding accuracy  
Improved patient information  
Better insurance information  
Improved claim submission controls

## Rejection Analysis

Rejected claims decreased from 65 in January to 52 in June.

A reduction in rejected claims can indicate improved front-end and claim submission processes.

Potential areas contributing to improvement include:

Eligibility verification  
Patient demographic validation  
Insurance verification  
Claim data validation  
Electronic gateway monitoring  
Pre-submission quality checks

## Denial Analysis

The number of denied claims decreased from 95 in January to 78 in June.

The denial rate also decreased:

January: 7.60%

June: 5.38%

The reduction indicates an improvement in the overall claim lifecycle.

Denial management should continue to focus on identifying recurring denial categories and addressing their root causes.

## Accounts Receivable Analysis

Total AR decreased from $485,000 in January to $412,000 in June.

This represents a reduction in outstanding receivables.

A reduction in AR may indicate:

Improved claim resolution  
Effective payer follow-up  
Improved denial recovery  
Faster payment processing  
Better AR prioritization

## AR Over 90 Days Analysis

AR over 90 days decreased from $72,000 in January to $48,000 in June.

This is an important improvement because aged AR represents accounts requiring additional follow-up and may carry increased collection risk.

The reduction suggests improved focus on older outstanding accounts.

## Collections Analysis

Collections increased from $398,000 in January to $485,000 in June.

The increase in collections, combined with the reduction in AR, indicates stronger revenue realization during the analysis period.

Potential contributing factors include:

Improved claim acceptance  
Reduced denials  
Effective AR follow-up  
Faster claim resolution  
Improved payment posting  
Timely payer follow-up

Provider enrollment issue

Missing documentation

## RCM Process Improvement Recommendations

### 1. Strengthen Eligibility Verification

Verify coverage and benefits before claim submission whenever applicable.

### 2. Improve Claim Validation

Use pre-submission checks to identify missing or inconsistent claim information.

### 3. Monitor Electronic Gateway Responses

Review acceptance and rejection responses promptly and correct rejected claims.

### 4. Strengthen Denial Management

Track denial categories, root causes, corrective actions, and recovery results.

### 5. Prioritize Aged AR

Focus AR follow-up on high-value and aging accounts, particularly balances over 90 days.

### 6. Monitor Payer Trends

Analyze payer-specific denials, rejections, processing times, and payment patterns.

### 7. Track RCM KPIs

Review key performance indicators regularly to identify operational changes and improvement opportunities.
